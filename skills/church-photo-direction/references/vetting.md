# Vetting a photo

Check every candidate. Reject on any "no".

## Fit

- **Tradition:** does the setting match the church? No saints' statues, icons or crucifixes for Baptist, nondenominational or Church of Christ congregations; no rock-concert stages for liturgical parishes; no robed clergy for churches without them; no wedding or ordination scenes unless that is the topic.
- **Setting:** small town vs city, climate, region. No skylines for rural churches, no palm trees for Minnesota, no European cathedrals for an American Baptist church.
- **People:** do they plausibly look like this congregation and the people it wants to reach (ages, ethnicities, families)? Prefer candid moments to posed studio shots.
- **Scale:** a 90-person church should not show arena crowds.
- **One building:** if the church's own building appears anywhere, never add stock photos of different church buildings; visitors will think it is the church.

## Content

- No visible logos, brand names, sponsor names (including in stained glass), readable signs of real organisations, or license plates. If the sign sits at an edge and the rest of the photo is strong, crop it out (`magick in.jpg -gravity west -crop 85%x100%+0+0 +repage out.jpg`, adjusting gravity and percentage) and check the result at desktop and phone crops; if it is central, reject the photo.
- No partially clothed people; crop portraits to shoulders and up.
- No children's faces in close-up unless the photo is clearly stock and the use is general; never caption stock children as the church's kids.
- No text baked into the image.
- No photo of a real, identifiable person captioned as a named staff member, pastor or member.

## Quality

- Sharp at the size it will be shown (source at least 1.5× the display width).
- Faces and the subject stay in frame at both desktop and phone crops (check `object-position`).
- Consistent light and treatment across the page (all warm daylight, or all graded black-and-white for 09).
