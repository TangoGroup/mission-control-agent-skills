A word from our pastor

Thank you for stopping by. Karen and I came to Pine Ridge in 2014, and what struck us first was how this church takes care of people. When someone is in the hospital in Rolla, there's a casserole on their porch before they get home. When a family moves in, somebody shows up with a pie.

We are a plain, Bible-teaching church. We sing the old hymns and a few new ones, we pray for each other by name, and we try to love this town well. You don't need to know anybody or have anything figured out. Just come as you are, and come hungry on Wednesdays.

DW

Your first Sunday

Here are the answers to what most folks wonder before they visit. If you'd like someone to meet you at the door, call the office and we'll be watching for you.

Where do I park?

Use our lot off Walnut Street, behind the church. Spaces near the side door are saved for guests and anyone who needs them.

Is the building easy to get around?

Yes. The Walnut Street entrance has no steps, and an elevator goes to the Fellowship Hall. Large-print bulletins and hearing assist devices are at the welcome table.

What should I wear?

Whatever you're comfortable in. You'll see suits and you'll see jeans. Nobody is looking.

What about my children?

The nursery (birth to age 3) is staffed by two screened volunteers every Sunday. Older kids are welcome in worship, and there's a children's sermon at the front.

How long is the service?

About an hour and ten minutes. We usually let out by five till noon, in time to beat the lunch crowd at the Main Street Diner.

Will I be singled out?

No. We'll shake your hand and tell you we're glad you came. There's a card in the pew if you'd like us to get in touch, and it's fine if you don't.

Our story

Twenty-three people started this church in the upstairs room of the Pine Ridge Mercantile. We've been on Main Street ever since.

  1. 1887

    First Baptist is organized with 23 charter members and a circuit-riding preacher who came once a month.

  2. 1896

    Members build the white frame sanctuary at Main and Walnut, mostly with lumber from the Harlan family mill.

  3. 1921

    The bell is hung in the new tower, paid for by a year of pie suppers.

  4. 1958

    The education wing and Fellowship Hall are added for a growing Sunday School.

  5. 2019

    The elevator, accessible entrance and new roof are dedicated, debt-free.
