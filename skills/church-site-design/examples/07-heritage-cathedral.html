Come to Pray, or Come to See

Some forty thousand people walk through the west doors each year. Some come for Sunday worship, some for the windows and the stone. Both are welcome, and many find they came for both.

For Worshippers

Your first Sunday at the Cathedral
  • Arrive by 10:15 for the 10:30 Eucharist; ushers at the west doors will hand you an order of service.
  • Children are welcome throughout. Nursery from 9:00; Sunday School at 9:15 in the Undercroft.
  • Coffee hour follows in Ellison Hall. Look for the clergy in black cassocks; they would like to meet you.

Cathedral Music

A Choir Tradition Since 1874

The Cathedral Choir of boys, girls and adult singers sings eight services a week in term, keeping the English choral tradition alive in the middle of an American city. The great Harrow organ of 1929, with more than five thousand pipes, has been restored twice and is played every day.

Our History

One Hundred & Fifty-Four Years

  1. 1872

    A parish for the mill city

    Founded by Scots and Welsh ironworkers as a mission chapel on Fifth Avenue, named for the Irish monk who carried the faith to Iona.

  2. 1891

    The great nave is consecrated

    The present building rises in Indiana limestone. It is named the cathedral of the diocese in 1903.

  3. 1907

    The Great West Window

    Thirty-four feet of glass telling the story of Creation, installed as a gift of the city's steelworkers.

  4. 1929

    The Harrow organ

    Dedicated the week before the Crash, and played every day through the Depression that followed.

  5. 1983

    Columba's Table opens

    Saturday breakfast begins in the Undercroft during the closing of the mills, and has never missed a week.

  6. 2022

    The sesquicentennial

    One hundred fifty years marked with a civic service and the launch of the restoration of the West Window.

Giving · Capital Campaign

The Next 150

Restoring the Great West Window

After more than a century of Pittsburgh weather, the lead that holds the West Window together is failing. Over the next three years, conservators will take down all 2,600 panels, clean and re-lead them by hand, and return them to the wall with protective glazing, so that the window can greet the city for another 150 years.
